# ICE staffing data analysis

## Results

After President Donald Trump's signature funding bill in 2025, Immigration and Customs Enforcement received an unprecedented amount of funding to expand its operations, including hiring more agents. In months, the agency more than doubled its capacity as experts raised concerns about proper vetting and training.

This is a data dive into federal workforce data from the Office of Personnel Management. We compiled all the monthly accessions and separations files available which went back to 2005. Data specifically for ICE starts August 2011 and our analysis runs through June 2026.

Here's what we found:

- Between January 2025 and June 2026, the Trump administration hired nearly 12,000 full-time ICE workers. That is more than the entire decade before. That includes 4,400 hires in the first half of 2026.
- Between January 2025 and June 2026, 4,800 workers left ICE. That includes 2,900 in the first half of 2026. 
- Typically the bulk of separations comes from retirements, but in 2026, those were overshadowed by the people who were terminated and those who quit. In just the first half of 2026, more people have quit than in any other full calendar year since 2012, the first available year of data. Terminations so far this year are higher than the whole decade before the second Trump administration combined.
- The median tenure for people who quit in 2026 was six months and under four months for those who were terminated.
- From those hired during the decade leading up to Trump's second term (2015-2024), a 33% had a graduate degree and another 31% had a bachelor's degree. The people hired since 2025 tend to have a lower education: those with a high school diploma or less are now the largest group, making up 38% of the hires.

For a full breakdown of our methodology, you can read and run the `analysis.R` file. 

You can also read our full investigation here: https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/investigations/2026/09/26/ice-workers-quit-in-record-numbers/90862284007/
